THE FAST INVERSE SQRT 0x5f3759df, then one step of Newton — 1/√x almost for free

The most famous magic number in code. To light a 3D world you need 1/√x for every surface normal, thousands of times a frame, and division-then-square-root was ruinously slow. The Quake III trick reinterprets the float’s bits as an integer, computes i = 0x5f3759df − (i >> 1), reads it back as a float — and that single subtraction lands astonishingly close to 1/√x. One Newton step then polishes it. The bit-seed exploits the IEEE-754 layout to approximate a logarithm; it is a reinterpret-cast that a strict f64 language cannot even name — so here we ground the half that does run: the Newton refinement.

THE TECHNIQUE y ← y·(1.5 − 0.5·x·y·y) — Newton polish of the magic seed

i13 is f64-only with no bit-reinterpret, so the 0x5f3759df seed is inexpressible here (disclosed). The demo grounds the Newton refinement that follows it, converging to 1/√x: live demo

RECOMMEND FOR I-13 the Newton half, on the compiler

  
On the canonical compiler, from a rough seed 0.4 the Newton step converges to 1/√4 = 0.5 in eight iterations:

  
$ i13 run tk_fastinvsqrt.i13   # y <- y*(1.5 - 0.5*x*y*y), x=4
RUN OK · 213 step(s) · peak stack 6 · call depth 9
x = 4   y0 = 0.4
inv = 0.5        -- converged to 1/sqrt(4)
close = 1        -- within 1e-7 of 0.5
# NOTE: the 0x5f3759df bit-seed needs float<->int reinterpret; i13 is f64-only, so it is not expressible here (disclosed).

  
Recommend as a marker, with an honest asterisk. This dart is the one place the batch cannot fully run: the celebrated part — the bit-reinterpret seed — is exactly the operation a strict f64 machine forbids, so we ground the Newton half and say so plainly (the disclosure is the point, not a patch). For the keeper question it is doubly out: the seed is an approximation (a different function from exact 1/√x, so not a same-function property), and the speedup is pure resource (B40). It earns its seat as a teaching case — where the machine’s honesty (no type-punning) meets the most famous trick in graphics.
