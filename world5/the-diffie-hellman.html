DIFFIE-HELLMAN two strangers agree on a secret in the open — g^ab = g^ba

Two people who have never met, shouting across a crowded room, can agree on a shared secret that the room cannot learn. Each picks a private number; each sends g^(private) mod p in the clear; each raises what they receive to their own private power. Because exponents commute — (g^a)^b = (g^b)^a = g^(ab) — both land on the identical secret, while an eavesdropper who saw only g^a and g^b would need the discrete logarithm to catch up. It is the 1976 idea that made public-key cryptography — and the modern internet — possible.

THE TECHNIQUE (g^a)^b = (g^b)^a = g^(ab) mod p — agreement in the open

The demo runs both sides with g=5, p=23: Alice and Bob exchange g^a and g^b, then each exponentiates to the same shared secret: live demo







  
HISTORY & CREDIT Diffie & Hellman · 1976 (Merkle)

  
“A shared secret must be sent secretly.” — not with commuting exponents: both sides derive it, the wire never carries it. cited

  
the exchange · Alice sends A = g^a, Bob sends B = g^b — both public.
the secret · Alice computes B^a, Bob computes A^b; both equal g^(ab).
1976 (GCHQ prior art) · Whitfield Diffie & Martin Hellman, with Ralph Merkle; earlier at GCHQ, James Ellis conceived non-secret encryption (1969) and Malcolm Williamson derived the key-exchange equivalent (1974, declassified 1997).

  
A secret two parties build from opposite sides and the wire never sees — secured by commuting exponents and the hardness of the discrete log. agreement

Recommend as a NULL — confluence-flavored, but a theorem underneath. Both parties converging on the same secret from opposite inputs looks like the seated confluence axis, and it is a beautiful agreement mechanism. But the convergence is exactly the commutativity of exponentiation — a theorem the group obeys (B39), and confluence is already seated. The secrecy rests on discrete-log hardness (a recognizer/complexity claim, B41). i13 grounds the agreement (both sides = 2). NULL — and the pair to secret-sharing: both are “agree without sharing,” one by algebra, one by threshold.
