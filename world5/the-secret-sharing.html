THE SECRET SHARING split a secret so any k of n rebuild it — and k−1 reveal nothing

Shamir’s secret sharing hides a secret as the constant term of a random degree-(k−1) polynomial and hands out points on the curve as shares. Any k shares pin the polynomial uniquely (Lagrange interpolation) and recover the secret; any k−1 shares leave it completely undetermined — every possible secret is still consistent. It is a threshold with a sharp edge: not “harder with fewer,” but all-or-nothing. Two properties live in the shares at once — any k agree on the same secret regardless of which k, and fewer than k carry zero information. That double structure is why this dart is a real keeper shot.

THE TECHNIQUE deg k−1 poly; any k points → same p(0); k−1 → nothing

The demo hides secret 42 in p(x)=42+5x+3x², then reconstructs p(0) from two different triples of shares — both return 42: live demo







  
HISTORY & CREDIT Adi Shamir · 1979

  
“To split a secret, give everyone a piece of it.” — then pieces leak. Shamir’s shares each reveal nothing, yet any k rebuild the whole. cited

the rebuild · any k points fix the polynomial uniquely (Lagrange) → the same p(0) — whichever k you pick.
1979 · Adi Shamir, “How to Share a Secret” — threshold cryptography’s founding scheme.

  
A secret dissolved into points where any quorum rebuilds it and no minority can — agreement from any subset, privacy from every small one. threshold

Recommend as the batch’s primary keeper shot — the strongest new-axis bid since the math pivot, and put to the panel unsoftened. Secret sharing carries two structures a plain “store the secret” mechanism lacks: (1) any-k-agree — different quorums reconstruct the same value, a confluence-flavored convergence-from-subsets; and (2) k−1 reveal nothing — a loose-spec family (any polynomial through s is a valid sharing). i13 grounds (1): two disjoint-ish triples both yield 42. The honest questions for the panel: is any-k-agree a new axis, or a duplicate of confluence (dart’s CRDT: replicas seeing the same set converge)? Is the threshold-privacy a real output-relation invariant, or does it fall to B44 (a property of the share encoding, moving under a change of polynomial) and B41 (a statement about what an adversary can compute)? My read leans NULL — confluence + encoding — but this is the genuine test of the near-complete claim, and it deserves the full adversarial panel.
