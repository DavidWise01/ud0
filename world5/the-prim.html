PRIM grow the tree from one seed — and reach the same minimum as Kruskal

Prim’s algorithm builds the same minimum spanning tree as Kruskal, but from the other direction: instead of picking cheap edges globally, it grows one tree from a starting node, repeatedly adding the cheapest edge that reaches a new vertex. Where Kruskal thinks in edges and forests joining, Prim thinks in a single expanding frontier — better when the graph is dense. And here is the quiet fact worth the dart: two different mechanisms, Kruskal’s global sort and Prim’s local growth, arrive at the same minimum weight (7). The MST weight is a property of the graph, not the method — witnessed, not enacted.
