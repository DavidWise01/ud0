THE NYQUIST LIMIT sample above twice the top frequency — or lose it forever

The sampling theorem draws a hard line: to capture a signal whose highest frequency is B, you must sample faster than 2B — the Nyquist rate. Below it, distinct frequencies become indistinguishable: a tone above half the sample rate masquerades exactly as one below, and no algorithm can tell them apart after the fact. Above it, the samples determine the continuous signal uniquely (Shannon’s reconstruction). It is why CD audio samples at 44.1 kHz for a 20 kHz ear, and why every analog-to-digital converter has an anti-alias filter in front of it.

THE TECHNIQUE fs > 2B to reconstruct; above fs/2, tones alias inseparably

The demo samples cosines of frequency 3 and 5 at fs=8 (Nyquist = 4): they produce identical samples — 5 is above fs/2 and masquerades as 3: live demo
