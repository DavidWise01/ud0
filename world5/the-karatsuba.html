THE KARATSUBA three multiplications where school taught four — the first sub-quadratic multiply

Split two numbers in half: x = a·B + b, y = c·B + d. Schoolbook needs four products (ac, ad, bc, bd). Karatsuba gets away with three: compute ac, bd, and (a+b)(c+d), then recover the cross term as (a+b)(c+d) − ac − bd. One multiply saved per split — and applied recursively it drops long multiplication from O(n²) to O(n^1.585). In 1960 it demolished Kolmogorov’s conjecture that multiplication must be quadratic, and opened the whole field of fast arithmetic.

THE TECHNIQUE z2=ac, z0=bd, z1=(a+b)(c+d)−z2−z0 — 3 mults

The demo multiplies 12×34 with three products (not four) and recombines them to 408, matching the true product: live demo

RECOMMEND FOR I-13 three-product multiply, on the compiler

  
On the canonical compiler, 12×34 via z2=ac, z0=bd, z1=(a+b)(c+d)−z2−z0 recombines to 408:

  
$ i13 run tk_karatsuba.i13   # 12*34, three products
RUN OK · 48 step(s) · peak stack 3 · call depth 0
z2 = 3    -- a*c
z0 = 8    -- b*d
z1 = 10   -- (a+b)(c+d) - z2 - z0
prod = 408   -- z2*100 + z1*10 + z0
same = 1     -- equals 12 * 34

  
Recommend as a NULL — the most historically important resource trick on the board. Karatsuba changed what was thought possible, not merely what was fast: sub-quadratic multiplication launched an entire field (Toom-Cook, Schönhage-Strassen, Harvey-van der Hoeven). And still: the product is identical — i13 prints 408 — only the multiply count falls. It is the definitive proof that even a complexity-class improvement is B40: a different mechanism for the same function, same bits out. NULL, and the batch’s headline case that “a better algorithm” is still resource, not axis.
