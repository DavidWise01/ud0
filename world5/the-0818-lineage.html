COURT — the throne that cannot be gamed rev1-0818b

the AUTHORITY that gates it

A game of 24 seats, two players and a throne. The throne is not a person — it is a grammar (i13): it accepts a legal move and refuses a broken one, and cannot be talked into anything because it does not reason. A wanderer leaves one seat empty each round so the game never freezes.

[ R | I·i13 | B ] ~~> 24 seats ~~> the wanderer ~~> never settles
LIT THE THRONE (i13) — R plays well-formed: ACCEPTED; B plays malformed: REFUSED. The referent I appears ZERO times — it is the authority that never plays, so it cannot be gamed. Grammar, not mind.
LIT THE WANDERER — one seat may abstain each round; the empty seat changes every other seat’s calculus, so the court never reaches a fixed point (55 configs in 200 rounds). Abstention is what keeps it alive.
AMBER the throne judges FORM, not TRUTH — a well-formed lie is accepted. i13 stops drift-toward-the-mode; it does not catch a coherent falsehood.

The industry find: RLHF, Constitutional AI, Debate, and GAN all use two players and a judge — and in every one the judge is a model, the same stuff as the players, so they can learn to game it. Here the judge is a language: frozen, deterministic, nothing to game. “The judge that cannot be gamed is the one that is not a mind.”
