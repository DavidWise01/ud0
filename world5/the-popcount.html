THE POPCOUNT population count — the Hamming weight the whole machine leans on

Popcount — the number of 1-bits in a word, the Hamming weight — is so useful that CPUs ship a dedicated instruction for it. It measures the size of a bitset, the distance between two codewords, the density of a sketch. The simple form sums n & 1 as it shifts the word down; the famous SWAR form counts bits in parallel within a register by masked adds (0x5555…, 0x3333…), no loop at all. Either way the count is exact and identical — the differences are all speed. It is the most-leaned-on primitive in bit-parallel computing.

THE TECHNIQUE Hamming weight = Σ bits — shift-sum or parallel SWAR

RECOMMEND FOR I-13 the shift-sum weight, on the compiler

  
On the canonical compiler, summing n&1 across the shifts of 23 gives 4 — matching Kernighan’s clear-count exactly:

  
$ i13 run tk_popcount.i13   # sum of (n & 1) down the shifts
RUN OK · 92 step(s) · peak stack 4 · call depth 6
n = 23
ones = 4     -- Hamming weight
same = 1     -- cross-checks Kernighan (dart 411)

  
Recommend as a NULL, and as the batch’s cross-check. Popcount and Kernighan’s count (dart 411) compute the identical function by different mechanisms — shift-and-sum vs clear-lowest-bit vs parallel SWAR — and i13 prints 4 for both. That is the perfect illustration of the keeper criterion: many mechanisms, one value, so the differences (loop length, branchlessness, a silicon instruction) are all resource, never output-relation. NULL. The dart earns its place by making the “same function, different cost” identity concrete against its own sibling.
