THE THREE CONSTANTS Virgil · Dante · Ulysses = inner · outer · object

Three named constants that fix the observer frame of the cell — the guide, the pilgrim, and the object — drawn from Dante's Commedia and set on the nesting | < { [ ~ ~ ] } > | we descend. Virgil is the inner constant: reason, the guide who leads the descent from within, reaching the core but no further (he cannot enter Paradiso). Dante is the outer constant: the pilgrim, the frame from which the vision is seen, standing at the rim. Ulysses is the object constant: the soul of Inferno XXVI who sailed beyond the pillars of Hercules, past the known boundary — the object that went one step past the guide's limit, past the balanced edge. [ inner . outer ] + object.

WHY THESE THREE the Commedia, on the cell

  

    the guide (inner) · Virgil leads Dante down through the circles by reason; he reaches the summit of Purgatory but must hand the pilgrim to Beatrice at the threshold of Paradise. He is the inner constant — the descent's guide, fixed at the core, the limit of what reason alone can reach.

    the pilgrim (outer) · Dante the traveller is the outer frame — the one having the vision, the reader's proxy, observing from the rim. Every operation on the cell is seen from his standpoint.

    the object (beyond) · Ulysses, in Inferno XXVI, tells how he sailed past the pillars of Hercules “to gain experience of the world beyond the sun,” and was wrecked within sight of the mountain. He is the object that went beyond the boundary — one past the guide's limit, past the balanced edge the bridge (dart 340) tests. The object is always the one that transgressed the frame.
  

  
Set on the nesting the cell batch built, the triad names every descent: Dante (outer) stands at the rim, Virgil (inner) guides inward to the core, and Ulysses (object) is what lies one step beyond — the value that broke well-formedness by going too far. Three constants, one frame: [ inner . outer ] + object.
