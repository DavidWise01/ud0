THE MORPHOLOGY OF THE TALE

Strip the wonder-tale of its names, its kings and its firebirds, and a fixed skeleton is left: 31 functions enacted by 7 roles, and the functions always fall in the same order — any may be omitted, none may be swapped. V. Ya. Propp turned narrative into a grammar, and the grammar is runnable: a tale is a subset of the 31 indices, and it is valid iff its functions ascend. The blue team builds the grammar; the red team scrambles it.

source V. Ya. Propp, Morphology of the Folktale (Leningrad: Academia, 1928; Eng. trans. L. Scott, 1958/1968) — Russian formalism, the structural grammar of the wonder-tale. Room: THE NOVEL. Rendered as structure, not quoted.

ROOTS — the formalist move history

Petrograd/Leningrad, the 1920s: the Russian Formalists (Shklovsky, Jakobson, the OPOJAZ circle) ask not what a tale means but how it is made. Propp takes ~100 Afanasyev wonder-tales and does something new: he ignores content (who does it, with what) and records only function — the act as a unit of the plot.

His claim, 1928: the dramatis personae change endlessly, but their functions are constant, few, and few in kind. Form over content — the structuralist turn, a generation before Levi-Strauss put a name on it.

5

THE 7 PERSONAE — roles, not characters LIT

The functions distribute across exactly seven spheres of action. A role is not a person: one character can fill several roles, and several characters can share one.

7

THE ORDERING LAW LIT

Propp’s central structural claim: the functions always occur in the same sequence. A tale never runs the wedding before the villainy, the return before the departure. What varies is which functions appear — omission is free, reordering is forbidden.

That reduces “is this a well-formed tale?” to one computable test: are the chosen function-indices strictly increasing? The centre engine is exactly that test.

9

THE WITNESS live

The blue team re-runs the law on load: a known in-order tale must be accepted, a known out-of-order tale must be rejected, and the counts must hold (31 functions, 7 roles). If red drops the ordering law (6), this badge is where it shows.

red team · attacks & breaks ◩
1

THE ADVERSARY — the reach of the law

WALL The ordering law is strong, and bounded. Propp built it from one genre — the Russian wonder-tale (Aarne-Thompson 300–749), roughly a hundred texts. It does not claim to govern the novel, the anecdote, the myth, or the modern short story. Move outside the wonder-tale and the fixed 31-order is no longer measured — it is an analogy.

The engine is exact on its domain and silent off it. The famous over-claim lives in that silence.

2

THE GRAVEYARD

“All stories are literally the same.” Cut. Propp’s grammar is a wonder-tale grammar — a strong result over one genre, then over-extended by pop-culture into “every story ever.” The engine only certifies wonder-tale form.

“There are exactly 7 plots / 1 monomyth.” Cut. That is Campbell/Booker downstream, not Propp. Propp counts 31 functions and 7 roles — not 7 plots.

“Order does not matter, only the ingredients.” Cut. Order is the whole claim. Same functions, wrong order → not a tale. The checker proves it live.

6

THE TAMPER — break it

The red move: drop the ordering law. Make the checker accept any order — ingredients only. Now a scrambled non-tale like [11,8,31] passes as “valid,” and the witness (9), which demands that exact sequence be rejected, turns red.

With the law dropped, “valid tale?” degenerates into “are these real functions?” — order stops meaning anything and every scramble passes. The attack is real, and the witness catches it.
