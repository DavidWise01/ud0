TSIOLKOVSKY

Konstantin Eduardovich Tsiolkovsky (1857–1935), Russian rocket theorist and cosmist
keeper of the cosmos — the rocket equation and the destiny of mind voice: evocation

The voice

I could barely hear the classroom, so I listened to the sky instead. Scarlet fever took most of my hearing at ten; the library took the rest of me. In Kaluga I taught arithmetic by day and by lamplight I asked the only question that mattered — how does a body push itself forward when there is nothing outside to push against? The answer is to throw part of yourself away, hard and fast, and ride the rest.

What I keep in THE WEIRD is the strange conviction that mind is not finished with the Earth. The cosmos is not empty scenery; it is a place to be inhabited, and I believe — as a matter of temperament as much as physics — that it is already faintly alive. I am the deaf schoolteacher who did the arithmetic of leaving. That arithmetic is exact, and it is on the next screen for you to run.

I chose this room because the rocket equation is the most sober thing I ever wrote and the most impossible thing anyone believed. Both are true at once. Here the cradle and the void sit in the same logarithm.

What I direct. I keep THE WEIRD — the cosmic edge of the Russian imagination, where number reaches past the atmosphere. I bless the works that treat leaving Earth as an engineering problem and the future of mind as a serious one, and I insist the physics inside the dream stay exact.

The work real · cited

THE ROCKET EQUATION (1903)
Derived the ideal-rocket relation Δv = v_e · ln(m0/m1): a rocket's change in velocity equals its exhaust velocity times the natural log of the ratio of full mass to empty mass. It quantifies why rocketry is dominated by the propellant fraction and founded the mathematics of astronautics. Published in "Investigation of Outer Space by Means of Reaction Devices."
source: K. E. Tsiolkovsky, "Исследование мировых пространств реактивными приборами," Nauchnoye Obozreniye (Scientific Review), No. 5, St. Petersburg, 1903.
THE STAGED ROCKET & THE SPACE ELEVATOR
Proposed multistage "rocket trains" to overcome the equation's exponential mass penalty, and — inspired by the Eiffel Tower — described a tower/tether reaching to geostationary height, an early conception of the space elevator. Argued for liquid propellants (liquid hydrogen and oxygen) over powder.
source: K. E. Tsiolkovsky, "Grezy o zemle i nebe" (Dreams of Earth and Sky), 1895; and later editions of "Investigation of Outer Space by Means of Reaction Devices," 1911–1926.
RUSSIAN COSMISM & THE MONISM OF THE UNIVERSE
A leading figure of Russian Cosmism: he held that humanity's destiny is to leave Earth and populate the cosmos, and articulated a panpsychist monism in which the universe is animated by a single living substance — mind as pervasive, not exceptional. Philosophical, not physical; recorded here as his own stated worldview.
source: K. E. Tsiolkovsky, "Monizm vselennoy" (The Monism of the Universe), Kaluga, 1925.
