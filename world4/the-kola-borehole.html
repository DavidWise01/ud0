THE KOLA BOREHOLE

On the Kola Peninsula near Murmansk, from 1970 to 1992, Soviet crews drilled the Kólskaya sverkhglubokaya — the deepest artificial point on Earth, 12,262 m. Legend grew a “well to hell.” This panel is runnable: it holds the geothermal gradient, T(z) = T₀ + g·z, and proves that the heat rose faster than predicted — and that the temperature, not the drill length, is what stopped the hole.

source Kola Superdeep Borehole (SG-3), 1970–1992, Murmansk Oblast; final depth 12,262 m; observed ~180 °C at bottom vs a ~100 °C conservative expectation; no basalt layer, water-bearing fractured granite and microfossils at depth. ◦ blue team · the record & the physics
1

ROOTS — Kola, 1970–1992 record

A Cold-War race to drill the crust. Where the Americans abandoned Project Mohole, the Soviets pressed on for 22 years at a single site on the Kola Peninsula, reaching 12,262 m in 1989 — still the deepest artificial point on Earth. The design target was 15,000 m; it was never met.

The bit had not run out of steel or budget in the ordinary sense — the rock itself changed. The record is AMBER: history, honestly held.

2

THE GRADIENT — heat rises with depth LIT

Earth’s interior is hot; temperature climbs with depth along a geothermal gradient: T(z) = T₀ + g·z. A conservative crustal figure of ~7–8 °C/km predicts roughly 100 °C at 12 km.

Kola’s surprise was measured, not imagined: the effective gradient ran near ~14 °C/km, giving ~180 °C at the bottom — roughly double the expectation. The gradient law is LIT; the excess is the story.

3

THE REAL FINDINGS — what was actually down there LIT

Seismology had predicted a basalt layer a few km down. Kola found none — instead fractured, water-bearing granite all the way. There was free water 6+ km deep (squeezed from the rock, sealed by impermeable strata) and microfossils — ancient plankton — recovered from great depth.

No hollow, no fire, no void: real geology, more surprising than the myth. The Conrad discontinuity was not a rock boundary but a change in the rock’s state. LIT.

6

WHY IT STOPPED — the plastic wall

WALL The limit was physical, not mythical. Above ~180 °C the granite behaves less like brittle rock and more like plastic; the borehole walls flow inward and the hole closes on the bit. Drilling became a fight against a self-healing wall — the deeper they went, the faster it sealed.

Why it matters. With a conservative 7.5 °C/km gradient the 180 °C wall would sit past 22 km — below even the 15 km target. Kola’s higher-than-expected heat brought that wall up to ~12.26 km. The temperature, not the depth, is the stopping cause.

7

THE GRAVEYARD — the “well to hell”

A recording of screaming souls from the pit. Dead as science. The “well to hell” audio is a documented hoax — a re-dubbed film soundtrack circulated in the 1980s–90s, never a downhole measurement.

The Soviets opened a gateway and welded it shut. Dead. No instrument logs heat as voices; a microphone at 180 °C records a temperature, not a torment.

A bottomless void / cavern of fire. Dead. The cores show solid, water-bearing granite — no cavity, no flame.

Standing: the geothermal gradient — measured, ~180 °C at 12.26 km, the heat that ended the hole. LIT
