BAKHTIN AND DIALOGISM

Dostoevsky did not write mouthpieces. His characters are autonomous, unmerged voices that argue with each other and with him and are never resolved. Bakhtin called this the polyphonic novel. This sphere makes it structural: tag a scene's utterances by voice, stance and who they address, then measure whether the voices are unmerged and whether they speak to each other rather than only up to the author.

source Mikhail Bakhtin, Problems of Dostoevsky’s Poetics (1929; expanded 1963). Room: THE NOVEL. The literary theory is interpretation (AMBER); the partition-and-graph structure is measured (LIT). No quotations fabricated.

ROOTS — Bakhtin, 1929/1963 history

Mikhail Bakhtin wrote the first version in 1929, on the edge of arrest and near-erasure under Soviet censorship; he revised and expanded it in 1963. Its claim: Dostoevsky invented a genuinely new kind of novel — one where the author does not stand above the characters and pronounce a verdict, but lets a plurality of independent consciousnesses sound together, unmerged.

He named the form polyphonic, borrowing the word for many independent melodic lines. The theory is interpretation; stamp it AMBER. What this sphere measures is only the structure underneath it.

5

MONOLOGY vs POLYPHONY the claim

Monologic: one authoritative voice. The others are subordinated — their stances reduce to the author’s thesis, and every utterance addresses the single authorial centre. A didactic tract, an allegory with a moral.

Polyphonic: two or more voices whose stances are distinct and unmerged (no voice’s stance is a relabelling of another’s), who address each other — the talk is between them, not only up to the author. Dostoevsky, at his own admission, does not settle who is right.

7

LINEAGE after Bakhtin

From this book runs a whole line: the dialogic imagination, heteroglossia, later reader-response and discourse analysis — all the idea that meaning lives between voices, not inside one.

In-world it ties the keeper DOSTOEVSKY himself — whom Bakhtin theorised — and the two most unmerged voices in the canon: the Grand Inquisitor (Ivan’s parable, never refuted by argument) and the underground man of Notes from Underground (a voice that will not be summed up).

the authorial voice · the wall ◨
1

THE AUTHORIAL VOICE subordination

the monologising move Let the author reach down and make every character’s stance equal his own thesis, and have everyone address him. The voices stop answering each other; they answer only up. Distinct stances collapse; the address graph becomes a star. The novel monologises.

This is exactly what Bakhtin says Dostoevsky refuses to do. The Inquisitor is never put down by an authorial reply — only by Alyosha’s silent kiss, which answers as a voice, not as a verdict.

2

THE GRAVEYARD

“Characters are the author’s mouthpieces — each one argues the writer’s point.” Cut. In the polyphonic novel the voices are autonomous and unresolved; the author’s own view is one voice among them, not the tribunal above them.

“A great novel speaks with one clear moral voice.” Cut. That is the monologic tract. Its opposite — unmerged voices addressing each other — is what the engine stamps LIT.

6

THE TAMPER — force the authorial thesis

Subordinate every voice in the Karamazov scene to the author’s thesis: overwrite each stance, point each address at the author. Watch the polyphony count collapse and the novel monologise.

With every stance forced to the author’s and every address aimed at the centre, the distinct unmerged voices collapse to one and the graph becomes a star. UNMERGED VOICES and DIALOGISM both turn red: Dostoevsky’s polyphony needs voices that stay unsubordinated.
